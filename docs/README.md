# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the core project management guidance for OctoAcme. OctoAcme manages work through a repeatable lifecycle: validate an opportunity, plan the delivery approach, execute and track progress, release safely, and then reflect on outcomes to improve. The process is designed to keep teams aligned on goals, ownership, risks, and quality while maintaining a clear path from idea to delivery.

OctoAcme relies on a clear distribution of responsibilities across project roles. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, and communication; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; developers implement and test work; QA validates acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. These roles work together in a defined rhythm of planning, standups, reviews, and stakeholder updates to keep work moving without losing visibility into key constraints.

## Project management process overview

OctoAcme begins with project initiation to confirm the business need, define success metrics, identify stakeholders, and decide whether the effort should move forward into planning. Once approved, the team turns the initiative into a backlog, a milestone plan, and a Definition of Done. During execution, work is managed through a project board, recurring team meetings, and PR workflows that favor small changes, review, and clear acceptance criteria. Progress is tracked through velocity, burndown, success metrics, and escalation of blockers when risks affect delivery.

Communication is a critical part of the process. Weekly PM and product alignment, regular standups, demos, and stakeholder updates keep everyone informed. Risk management and incident communication follow a structured escalation path, with issues moving from the team to project leadership and, when needed, to sponsors or security responders. Quality is reinforced through automated CI checks, tests, security scans, manual QA when necessary, and release readiness reviews that confirm deployed changes are safe and observable.
